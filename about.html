    About

    What this planner does and how it works.

    Purpose

    Campus Life Planner is a small web app for students who need one place to keep track of assignments, study sessions, club activities and campus logistics. Every task carries a title, a category, a duration in minutes and a due date, which is enough to answer the questions that actually matter: what is due next, how much time have I planned, and am I over my weekly target?

    It is built with plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript with no framework and no build step. All of your data is stored on your own browser via localStorage. There is also a service worker caches the shell so the app still opens when you are offline on campus.

    Main Features

    • Six real pages: Dashboard, Board, Table, Calendar, About and Settings, each with its own URL and a single top-level heading.
    • Dashboard: total tasks, completion rate, total planned time, top category, a Chart.js 7-day creation trend and a weekly-target meter.
    • First-run setup: five quick steps for your name, theme, planning preferences, sample tasks or a fresh start, and a short wrap-up. Skippable, and editable later in Settings.
    • Board: drag a card into any column, or use the Move to button; keyboard and touch are both first-class.
    • Table: sortable on every column (aria-sort is exposed) with live regex search and <mark> highlighting.
    • Calendar: month grid keyed to due dates, plus a flat list of the month's tasks for screen-reader users.
    • Settings: units, weekly target, editable categories, theme, image storage, and JSON / CSV import & export.
    • Google Calendar: push tasks to Google Calendar through a pre-filled event link per task, or a bulk .ics download you can import.
    • Image attachments: optionally attach one picture to a task and everything is kept on this device only.
    • Validation: six blocking regex rules plus three advanced ones (back-reference, negative lookahead and lookbehind) with inline error messages.
    • Persistence: every change auto-saves; imports are structurally validated before anything is replaced.

    Regular-expression catalog

    Regular expressions used for validation and search
    Rule Pattern Why
    Title shape /^\S(?:.*\S)?$/ Forbids leading and trailing spaces.
    Messy whitespace /\s{2,}|[\t\n\r\f\v]/ Catches runs of spaces and stray tabs or newlines.
    Duration /^(0|[1-9]\d*)(\.\d{1,2})?$/ Non-negative, at most two decimals, no leading zeros.
    Date /^\d{4}-(0[1-9]|1[0-2])-(0[1-9]|[12]\d|3[01])$/ YYYY-MM-DD with a real month and day range.
    Category /^[A-Za-z]+(?:[ -][A-Za-z]+)*$/ Letters, single spaces and hyphens only.
    Duplicate word
    back-reference    		 /\b(\w+)\s+\1\b/i Catches study study | \1 repeats the captured word.
    Dangling preposition
    negative lookahead     		/^(?!.*\b(?:for|to|with|and|the|of|in|on|at)\s*$).+$/ Rejects a title that ends on a stop word, e.g. Prepare notes for.
    Time token
    lookbehind    		 /(?<!\d)\d{2}:\d{2}(?!\d)/ Finds clock times such as 16:00 without matching inside 116:003.
    Exact category ^Academics$ Whole-field match, used by the sidebar category filter.

    Keyboard map

    Keyboard shortcuts
    Key Action
    Tab / Shift+Tab Move through every interactive element, in DOM order.
    Enter / Space Activate a link, button or board card; submit the task form.
    Ctrl/Cmd + K Jump straight to Quick find. Works even while you are typing in a field.
    / The same shortcut, when not typing.
    N New task dialog (ignored while typing in a field).
    Esc Close the open dialog, close the mobile navigation drawer, or skip the first-run setup.
    Enter in setup Advance to the next step.
    Tab inside a dialog Focus is trapped inside the dialog until it is dismissed.
    Enter on a board card Activates its Move to button to allow you to move it to different columns.

    Accessibility notes

    • Every page has a skip link, one <h1>, and header/nav/main/footer landmarks.
    • Navigation is real links, so it works without JavaScript and announces the current page via aria-current="page".
    • The sidebar collapses to an icon rail on desktop. Labels are clipped rather than hidden, so their names are still announced.
    • Ctrl/Cmd+K jumps to search from anywhere, including from inside a form.
    • Sortable headers expose aria-sort; the horizontally scrollable table is a focusable labelled region.
    • Status updates use role="status" / aria-live="polite"; exceeding the weekly target switches to an assertive alert.
    • Validation errors sit next to their input, are linked with aria-describedby, and use role="alert".
    • The styling is deliberately plain with one accent colour, a neutral scale, thin borders, and shadows only where something genuinely sits above the page.
    • Icon-only buttons carry sr-only text; colour is never the only signal (priority also shows a text label, overdue dates also say “Overdue”).
    • Charts. The 7-day bars are a <canvas>, which is opaque to assistive technology, so the same numbers are exposed as a real list underneath.
    • Nothing is drag-only. Every board card keeps a Move to button, so the board is fully operable from the keyboard; dropping announces the result in a live region.
    • The first-run setup is a proper modal dialog: focus is trapped, Esc skips it, and each step is announced politely.
    • prefers-reduced-motion disables every transition and animation.
